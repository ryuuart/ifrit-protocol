# Surface lobes

Thirteen balls of one size under one scene, each wearing one of the optional
lobes of the physically based surface beside a neighbour that states
nothing more, and a page lit by the same scene.

The upper row, left to right:

- wine cloth, then the same cloth with a pale **sheen** — a soft light
  along the rim, strongest where the light from behind grazes it;
- steel, then the same steel **brushed** around its equator, then brushed
  a quarter turn over it from pole to pole — the highlight drawn out into
  a band along the brushing, turned with it;
- polished titanium, then the same metal under a **thin film** of 320
  nanometres and of 560 — the reflection tinted, and tinted differently
  by the two thicknesses and from the middle to the rim.

The lower row, left to right:

- blue plastic, then the same plastic at a tenth of its **specular**
  strength — its highlight and its bright rim gone — then at full strength
  tinted orange;
- a matte red, then the same red under a **clear coat**, for reference.

The page lies tilted back at the lower right. Its upper band is velvet
stating a sheen, its lower band the same velvet stating none; the word
across the upper band is steel brushed along the page's width, the word
across the lower band the same steel polished. Compose paints the page as
a surface's maps, and the maps of the sheen and the brushing exist only
because its materials state them.

The loop is 8 seconds and closes on itself. The key light swings across
the front of the rows and back, every ball turns once about its own axis
— carrying the brushings round with it — the lamp over the page slides
across it, and the page turns a little either way.

The balls are a generated sphere, so the brushing around the equator
converges at the poles into a knot, and a strongly brushed highlight shows
the triangles it is read across. There is no ground and no shadow, the
balls carry no labels, and nothing is interactive.
