# Glass forms

Glass and liquid forms standing in front of a patterned wall and three
coloured bodies, so what is behind each form is seen bent through it.
The wall is a black-and-cream checker crossed by bands of colour, drawn
unlit; a red cube, a blue ball and a yellow ring stand between it and the
glass.

The upper row, left to right, four balls:

- **clear glass** at an index of 1.5, the wall shrunk and turned over
  through it;
- **water**, the same ball at 1.33, bending less;
- **frosted glass**, the clear ball at a roughness of 0.4, the wall behind
  it blurred;
- glass at 1.5 that **disperses**, each colour bent by its own index, so
  every edge seen through it fringes red and blue.

The lower row, left to right:

- a **tinted slab** of green glass 20 units deep, turned, its medium
  leaving the green colour after 45 units;
- the same green glass **70 units deep**, darker and greener;
- a curved **amber shell** of liquid at 1.36;
- a square of **heat haze**: no colour of its own, the wall behind it
  displaced by a two-channel noise map that fades to still at the square's
  edge, its strength breathing.

The loop is 8 seconds and closes on itself. The eye drifts left and right,
the cube and the ball slide across behind the glass in opposite
directions, the cube turns, the ring rises and falls, and the haze swells
and settles — so the refraction slides through every form.

One layer of refraction: a form shows the opaque scene behind it and never
another form. Nothing is cast through the glass — no caustics, no coloured
shadows — and nothing here casts a shadow at all. The refraction is read
from the picture of the scene, so where a bent ray leaves the picture the
studio's panorama stands in. Nothing is interactive.
