# Form / System — Vormgevers

A native lettering study based on Wim Crouwel's **Vorm Gevers** exhibition
poster for the Stedelijk Museum Amsterdam (1968), held by MoMA.

The poster's exposed construction grid, inverted upper field, stepped
lowercase forms and two oversized words determine the composition. A large
letter g, its receding contour drawings, a citron printing register and an
animated disassembly extend the source into a kinetic construction study.
The small museum heading uses Helvetica Neue; the display words are drawn
outlines, not substitute font glyphs. This is an interpretation of the
lettering and layout rather than a facsimile.

Each letter starts as occupied square cells. Native contour union removes
internal edges, optical joints connect the diagonal steps, and corner
rounding creates continuous strokes. The same outline supplies the filled
letter, its wire drawings and six clipped printing strips. The strips
separate and return while the construction grid stays fixed. A second
registration of the word moves over the ink through multiply compositing.

Open `form_system.cpp` in Sketchbook. The declared still is at 3.4 seconds.

Reference:
[Wim Crouwel, Vorm Gevers, 1968 — MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/8735).
The museum image is used for visual comparison and is not bundled in the sketch.
